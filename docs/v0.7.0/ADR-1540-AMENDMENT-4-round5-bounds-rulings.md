### Correction 1 to the panel — RP-2's widening does **nothing at all** at the present population, and the panel states a future-tense defect in the present tense

RP-2 argues that AM-22's widening from 100 to 500 *"strictly reduces the evaluated coverage of recent records"* because *"the 400 newly-admitted records are, by construction, older."* **There are no 400 newly-admitted records.** The list bound is a bound *on* the population, not the population: the candidate set is **97** (architect, 2026-09-15), **98** (`pm-agent`, 2026-09-16), re-derived a third time by the panel itself (§2 Result 3). At a population of 98, a bound of 100 and a bound of 500 fetch **the identical 98 records**, and a ceiling of 100 covers all of them. **Widening displaces nothing today and the interaction RP-2 describes has, at this moment, zero effect.**

**I am not using that to dismiss it, and the reason is the more important half.** The defect **arms the moment the population exceeds the ceiling**, and the population is **two records away** — the same distance, in the same week, that AF-8 measured for the list bound it was issued to close. So RP-2 is a correct finding about a state this repository reaches in days, stated as an observation of a state it is not in. **The consequence for the ruling is real:** the panel frames the fix as *"undo or rebalance the widening"*, and at a population of 98 there is nothing to undo. **The thing that is actually wrong is the tie-break, which RP-2 names in passing and treats as context** — and which turns out to be a documented product policy nobody in this chain noticed (§0.1 row 2). **AM-29 rules the tie-break, not the widening.**

### Correction 2 to the panel — RP-7's stated attack does not produce exit 2, and the real cause is worse and needs no attacker

RP-7 constructs *"an issue with a pathological event history — thousands of cross-references, bot comments, label churn — that reliably times out or errors on `/events`."* **Under the design as written, a long event history does not error.** TD §3 row **F14** and **AM-5** bind the events query to 10 pages / 1000 events, and *"exhausting the page bound without a match is **not** an error"* — the record is **gated**, exit 0, with a `WARN`. So the panel's constructed input produces the *designed* outcome, not exit 2. Separately, `commented` is not an issue **event** at all — it is a timeline entry — so "bot comments" do not inflate the list the gate reads.

**What does produce exit 2 at D5.3 is any transport failure: a 5xx, a timeout, a transferred or deleted issue — and, dominantly, rate-limit exhaustion.** And that is the finding the panel reached for and missed: **RP-4 is the cause of RP-7.** A ceiling denominated in *checks* permits 100 checks × up to 10 pages = **up to 1,000 requests per cycle**; at the documented five-minute cadence that is **up to 12,000 requests/hour against a 5,000/hour installation limit** (`docs/OVERSIGHT-RUNBOOK.md:56`; the limit is stated in AM-19 and TD §2.2.1). Once the limit is exhausted, `gh api` exits non-zero, D5.3 exits 2, **and because FR7 forbids caching, the next cycle re-spends the same budget from scratch and exhausts it again.** A rate-limit-driven exit 2 is **self-perpetuating** while the queue is large.

**And it does not need the tail case.** At 100 checks per cycle and 288 cycles per day, the gate issues ~28,800 events queries per day. A 0.1% transient-failure rate — unremarkable for any HTTP API — aborts **~29 cycles per day**, each one silently producing no new issue work. **D5.3's exit 2 will fire routinely from ordinary transience, with no attacker at all.** That is a far stronger case for RP-7 than the one the panel made, it is arithmetic rather than construction, and it is why AM-31 rules the way it does.

## 1. Rulings

### AM-29 — **RP-2, THE RULING. The defect is that AM-19 collapsed two different orders into one. The order the gate WALKS is a REACHABILITY decision and must favour recently-acted-on records; the order the gate EMITS is a FAIRNESS decision, it is `#901`'s documented FIFO-within-band, and it does not change.** (Amends **AM-19** point 2 and **AM-22**; restores **Step E**'s sort to load-bearing; **no product-visible change**.)

In one sentence, because the sentence is the ruling:

> **Walk the candidate set in `(rank, most-recently-updated first, number ascending)` order; emit the eligible set in `(rank, number ascending)` order — the order `next_candidates.jq` already documents — and never let the walk's tie-break be "simplified" into the emission's.**

**Why the two are different decisions, which is the whole finding.** Prefix-correctness's load-bearing content is **rank dominance**: *nothing omitted can outrank anything emitted.* That property holds for `(rank, X)` under **any** total `X`, because rank is the first key and every rank-0 record is walked before any rank-1 record. **Within a band, "outrank" is undefined** — the records are of equal priority — so the within-band tie-break is not a correctness choice at all. It decides two unrelated things that AM-19 accidentally fused:

- **In the walk, the tie-break decides which records are ever *evaluated*** when the budget runs out. That is a **reachability** decision, and under `number` ascending it is the worst available one: a record's number never changes, so **nothing the human can do moves a starved record forward** except re-labelling its priority. There is no recovery act.
- **In the emission, the tie-break decides which issue the worker *builds first*.** That is a **fairness** decision, it is `#901`'s deliberate FIFO-within-band (`next_candidates.jq` header, quoted at §0.1), and **it is not mine to reverse.**

AM-19 made Step E *"a no-op on a correct implementation"* by walking in the emission order, then told the designer to keep it anyway as a tripwire (TD `:722`). AM-22 then widened the set, and the walk's tie-break silently became the thing that decides which records are ever looked at. **That is the seam. Two rulings, 55 lines apart, and the property that fell between them was never written down in either.**

**Binding:**

1. **The walk's order is `(rank, updated_at DESC, number ASC)`** — rank first and unchanged; `updated_at` descending within a band; `number` ascending as the final key so the order is strict and total with zero ties (issue numbers are unique). **If the designer verifies that `updated_at` is not reliably present on the Step C payload, the binding falls back to `(rank, number DESC)`**, which achieves the same *direction* (newly-admitted older records sort behind, not ahead) with no new field. **The designer MUST verify which applies and record the verification; they MUST NOT assume.**
2. **The emission order is `(rank, number ASC)`, unchanged, and Step E's sort is now LOAD-BEARING rather than redundant.** It is the single line that converts the walk order into `#901`'s documented output contract. **Binding: §6.2 gains a test asserting that the emitted order is `(rank, number ASC)` even when the walk order differs** — the one test that would catch a future edit "simplifying" Step E away on the grounds that AM-19 called it a no-op. Amendment 3's own risk register warned about optimising the ranking; this is the same hazard with the opposite sign.
3. **AM-19's four-limb prefix-correctness proof is UNAFFECTED and is not re-opened.** The panel's §4 rejection 1 established that the proof is sound over a strict total order; `(rank, updated_at, number)` is a strict total order; the proof carries verbatim. **This ruling changes which total order, not whether there is one.** §3 of the panel's artifact is not re-derived.

**Why this closes RP-2 rather than relocating it.** RP-2's harm is the starvation of **recently-authorized** issues. Under AR-7 the authorizing act is exactly one thing — a human applying the dispatch label from their own account — and **applying a label updates the issue's `updated_at`.** So under this ruling **the act that authorizes a record is the same act that moves it to the head of its band.** Under `number` ascending it was not, and there was no second act that would. That is the property the cluster was missing, and it costs one sort key.

### What AM-29 is **NOT**

- **NOT a change to what the worker builds.** The emitted list, and therefore `bin/hos-cron`'s `head -5` and the worker's choice, is ordered exactly as it is today. **`#901`'s FIFO-within-band is preserved byte-for-byte, and there is no product-visible behaviour change and no H1 item to add.** I considered ruling the emission order to recency as well and **declined**: it would reverse a documented product policy, which is not an architect's call, and it would have cost a human question this chain does not need.
- **NOT a claim of liveness.** It gives a **recovery act** and a **promotion-on-authorization** property; it does not guarantee that every record is eventually evaluated. Under a population much larger than the ceiling, a record nobody touches sinks. That is a bounded, stated residual (**R-8**, below), not a guarantee I am pretending to.
- **NOT free of a new attack surface, and I state it rather than let it be found.** **NEW RESIDUAL R-8:** an untrusted author can raise their own record's walk position by editing their issue, because an edit bumps `updated_at`. **Bound on `technical-design`: R-8 is recorded in §7.2 with these three mitigations attached** — (i) the record must already be in the target milestone **and** carry the dispatch label to be in the candidate set at all, which requires a triage write the author does not have; (ii) **rank dominates recency absolutely**, so `priority:critical` is never displaced by any amount of churn in a lower band; (iii) the human's authorizing act is itself an update, so it always lands at the head of its band. **The trade is deliberate: a deterministic, permanent starvation with no recovery act, for an attacker-influenceable, transient one with two.** That is the better trade and it should be recorded as a trade.

### AM-30 — **RP-4, THE RULING. E-8 is real, and the defect is larger than the number: the ceiling is denominated in the wrong UNIT. A "check" is 1–10 requests, so a ceiling of 100 checks is a ceiling of 100–1,000 requests — which exceeds the hourly rate limit at the documented cadence. The bound is RE-DENOMINATED in GitHub API requests and held at ≈100 per cycle. AM-19's "the ceiling must not bind in normal operation" is WITHDRAWN as unsatisfiable.** (Amends **AM-19** point 5 and its cost paragraph; amends **AM-20** point 5's report class; **does not raise the ceiling**.)

**The designer escalated E-8 rather than absorbing it and was right to. The panel resolved it as "real and understated." Both are correct and both stop one step short.** AM-19 derived **100** from *"one check per record on one list page, i.e. the natural per-cycle maximum"*; AM-22, 55 lines later, made the list five pages; the broken derivation propagated into Amendment 3's own CONFIDENCE block (`:381`) and into the design. **But repairing the derivation by re-deriving the same number would leave the deeper defect in place, and the deeper defect is the unit.**

**The arithmetic, stated so it can be checked rather than believed.** AM-5 bounds the events query at **10 pages per record**. A "check" is therefore **1 to 10 requests**, not 1. A ceiling of 100 *checks* is a ceiling of **100 to 1,000 requests** per cycle. At the documented five-minute cadence (12 cycles/hour) that is **1,200 to 12,000 requests/hour against a 5,000/hour installation limit.** **A bound whose unit is not the rationed resource cannot bound the rationed resource.** This is also precisely why H1 item 2's **≈105 requests/cycle** is, as the panel says, *a floor presented to the human as a ceiling* — and re-wording the disclosure would have been the wrong fix, because the number is false for a structural reason.

**Binding:**

1. **The gate's cost bound is denominated in GitHub API requests spent per cycle, not in authorization checks**, and it is set at **≈100 requests** — derived from the rate limit and the cadence (≈1,200/hour, ≈24% of the installation's 5,000/hour, leaving the rest of the worker's and the overseer's budget intact), **not** from a page size. The flag's name and its counter's semantics follow; **"may only lower" is preserved unchanged**, and the value is **not raised** — it is the same number with a real unit behind it.
2. **AM-19's requirement that *"the ceiling must not bind in normal operation"* is WITHDRAWN.** It is unsatisfiable: at a population of 98, ≈95–103 requests per cycle, the ceiling is at its boundary **today**, and the population grows monotonically while the rate limit does not. **It is replaced by the honest requirement: the ceiling WILL bind in normal operation, and what the design must prove is that the gate is correct when it binds** — which is AM-29's job, and which is why the two are one ruling in two parts.
3. **Because a binding ceiling is now the normal state, AM-20's report classes split** (amending AM-20 point 5): `unevaluated:cost-ceiling` is **routine** and belongs on the summary line and the `UNEVALUATED` line only — **it MUST NOT emit a `WARN`.** `unevaluated:list-truncated` and the new `unevaluated:query-failed` (AM-31) are **anomalies** and keep the loudest class. **A `WARN` that fires on every cycle of correct operation is not a warning; it is a banner, and by the second week it is invisible** — which is AM-24's own argument, applied to AM-24's own author.
4. **H1 item 2's cost bullet is corrected in §3(b) of this document**, so the human is asked to clear a true ceiling rather than a floor. See §3(b) and the note there on `pm-agent`'s *"change nothing else in H1."*
5. **The lever is unchanged and is restated because it is now load-bearing rather than precautionary.** This cost is **FR7's** price. The gate's cost is **O(population) per cycle** while the rate limit is fixed, so **no choice of bounds is stable against a growing queue** — bounds relocate the problem, they do not resolve it. **The only lever that resolves it is FR7's "never cache an authorization", and that is `pm-agent`'s and the human's, not mine.** Any future proposal to change the ceiling in either direction MUST cite this paragraph and say what it is trading. §4 recommends it be filed now rather than discovered at the limit.

### What AM-30 is **NOT**

- **NOT a raise.** The number does not move. Its *unit* and its *derivation* move, and one falsified requirement is withdrawn. A reviewer who reads this as licence to raise the ceiling has inverted it: the whole point is that the old figure was **unbounded above** and is now bounded.
- **NOT a claim that the cost is now safe forever.** It is safe at this population and this cadence. Point 5 is the statement of what happens after that, and it is routed rather than absorbed.
- **NOT a re-opening of AM-5.** The 10-page events bound stands exactly as written; this ruling prices it, it does not change it.

### AM-31 — **RP-5 and RP-7, RULED TOGETHER. AM-21's two-limb criterion is REPLACED. The exit code is decided by WHICH HARM the incompleteness creates, not by whether the omission is enumerable and ordered. Security-affecting → exit 2. Completeness- or ordering-affecting → exit 0 and report. D5.3 therefore QUARANTINES the unqueryable record and CONTINUES the walk; Step C's truncation exits 0 on a stated ground rather than "under protest".** (**REPLACES AM-21**; amends TD §3 rows **F13** and the Step C bound; confirms **F1 … F12** and the Step B exits unchanged.)

**The panel's diagnosis is right and it is the best sentence in the artifact:** *"fail closed" was applied as a slogan rather than per-failure-mode.* AM-21 was written to fix that and did not finish, and the reason it did not finish is visible now: **it asked what the gate KNOWS about the omission (enumerable? ordered?) and never asked what the omission COSTS.** Two bounds that both fail the same test can carry opposite harms, and a criterion blind to the harm will keep producing a rule that is too strict in one place and too loose in another — which is exactly the state RP-5 and RP-7 describe from opposite sides.

> **THE REPLACEMENT CRITERION (binding).** **A failure or bound that can affect the *security* determination — whether any record is treated as authorized — MUST exit 2.** **A failure or bound that affects only the *completeness* or the *ordering* of the emitted set MUST exit 0, and MUST report the omission: what class, how much (or that the extent is unknown), and why.** Fail-closed means *never authorize on an unknown*; it does not mean *stop the world on an unknown*, and the difference is the whole of this ruling.

**Applied, per failure mode:**

- **Step B — configuration** (empty `BOT_ACCOUNTS`, no individual human CODEOWNER, unreadable CODEOWNERS or roster, malformed arguments). A wrong trust, bot or CODEOWNER set changes **every** record's determination and can do so in the permissive direction. **Security-affecting → exit 2, unchanged. F1 … F12 stand exactly as written.**
- **Step C — the list bound.** No truncated record can be wrongly *authorized*; some can be wrongly *omitted*. **Completeness-affecting → exit 0 + the loudest report.** RP-5's objection — that the concession fails both limbs, not one — **is answered by replacing the criterion it fails, not by strengthening the exit.** The design must stop presenting this as an exception granted under protest; it is the rule. **And RP-5's second half is upheld: the gate MUST NOT state a truncation count it cannot derive.** GitHub's `/issues` returns no total, so the report says *"at least 500 records matched; the total is unknown"* — **never a number the gate cannot compute.** (A `Link: rel="last"` probe could bound it; that is an optional improvement, **not** binding, and it must not become a reason to delay.)
- **D5.3 — the events query fails for one record mid-walk. THIS IS THE ONE THAT CHANGES.** The determination is **per-record**: one query per record, no shared state, and its failure direction is *not authorized*. **A failed events query on record X cannot corrupt record Y's determination, and it cannot authorize X.** So exit 2 there buys **zero** security and costs **all** new issue work. **Binding: the record is quarantined — counted under AM-20's existing `unevaluated` machinery with a new token `unevaluated:query-failed`, never counted as `gated`, reported in the anomaly class with the issue number — and THE RANKED WALK CONTINUES.** This is the third option the panel names, AM-20 already built the vocabulary for it, **and `probe.py:298-303` already implements it in shipped code** (§0.1, Correction 4). The gate's exit 2 was the novel behaviour, not the quarantine.
- **What D5.3's exit 2 was actually protecting, named so it is not lost.** AM-21's stated reason — *"a partial list can silently demote a `priority:critical` item"* — is an **ordering** concern, not a security one, and AM-21 paid for it with a total stop. Under AM-20 the demotion is no longer silent: `unevaluated:query-failed` names the record and `complete=no` says the answer is partial. **The concern is real and it is now met by reporting instead of by halting.**

**Why this does not weaken the gate, stated in the direction a reviewer will suspect.** An attacker who can make one record's events query fail causes that record to be **not selected** — fail-closed, for that record only. An attacker who can make *every* record's events query fail causes `eligible=0, complete=no` — fail-closed for everything, loudly reported, with the cycle proceeding to its other work. **There is no input to this path that produces an authorization.** What changes is that ordinary transience (§0.1, Correction 2: ~29 cycles/day at a 0.1% failure rate) stops silently deleting a cycle's work selection.

### What AM-31 is **NOT**

- **NOT a relaxation of Step B.** Every configuration exit stays exit 2. A reader who generalises "quarantine and continue" to Step B has removed the gate's foundation.
- **NOT permission to continue past an *unknown-extent* corruption of the candidate set.** If the gate cannot tell whether the list query returned records at all (F12 — non-zero exit, non-JSON, not a list), that is exit 2, unchanged: there is no set to walk and no determination to report.
- **NOT a retirement of AM-21's insight, only of its criterion.** AM-21 was right that the design was arguing both sides without a stated principle. It supplied a principle that measured the wrong thing. **The principle is replaced; the demand for one is upheld.**

### AM-32 — **The consequence of AM-31 that would otherwise be missed: an incomplete exit-0 answer MUST NOT be indistinguishable from a complete empty one at the CALLER.** (Amends **AD-3**'s call-site contract; binding on TD §2.4 and §2.5.)

Under AM-31, a total events failure now returns **exit 0 with an empty list** where it previously returned exit 2. At `bin/hos-cron:1137-1163` those are **opposite** signals: exit 2 sets `_gate_candidates_ok=0` → `_gate_all_ok=0` → the cycle **proceeds** on "unknown"; exit 0 with empty output leaves `_gate_candidates_ok=1`, and `:1158-1163` then **skips the entire cycle as "no actionable work"** if the other gates are also empty. **AM-31 would, unamended, convert "the gate could not determine anything" into "there is nothing to do" — a silent skip, which is the precise failure class `CLAUDE.md`'s sandbox section names as the one to guard against, one layer up.**

**Binding: the gate MUST make `complete=no` machine-distinguishable to its caller, and `bin/hos-cron` MUST NOT treat an incomplete determination as "no actionable work."** Only `eligible=0 AND complete=yes` may contribute to the skip condition. **The mechanism is the designer's** — a third exit status (`2` is taken by fail-closed and by `argparse`; `3` is free), or a token the caller greps from the summary line. **What is binding is that the caller can tell, and that a test asserts it at the call site** — in `tests/automation/test_hos_cron.py`, which AM-28(a) already added to the change set for exactly this class of call-site contract.

**What AM-32 is NOT:** not a new field in the summary line — `complete=<yes|no>` already exists (AM-20). It is the requirement that the **caller acts on it**, which AM-20 specified for the operator and never specified for `bin/hos-cron`.

### AM-33 — **RP-6. The fifth limb is real. Prefix-correctness is RESTATED with its scope attached, and the design MUST stop claiming a property over the repository that it holds only over the fetched set.** (Amends **AM-19**'s statement of the property; binding on TD §2.3 and §6.2.)

The adversarial lens reconstructed AM-19's four-limb proof and **could not break it** (panel §4, rejection 1) — a real result, and I do not re-derive it. RP-6 is not an attack on the proof; it is the observation that the proof's **input** is a truncated set. Step C truncates by `created desc` **before** ranking, so an omitted record can outrank an emitted one, and the emitted list is a prefix of *the fetched order*, not of *the complete order*.

**Binding:**

1. **The property is restated with its scope: for any stop point `k`, the emitted list equals the first `k` elements of the ranked walk over the candidate set Step C returned — and it equals the first `k` of the complete answer if and only if `complete=yes`.** The design must say this in those words. **An unqualified claim of prefix-correctness is a claim the gate cannot make and must stop making.**
2. **The two halves of the property are named separately, because they have different owners and different failure modes.** **Rank dominance** (nothing omitted outranks anything emitted) is a **safety** property of the walk and is preserved by any `(rank, X)` order — AM-29 depends on this. **Input completeness** is a property of Step C and is **not** preserved when the list bound binds. Conflating them is what let a four-limb proof be presented as covering five.
3. **§6.2 gains the fifth limb's test, and it is an absence test:** when the list bound truncates, the gate MUST report `complete=no` and MUST NOT emit any assertion of global prefix-correctness. **A four-limb suite stays green while the fifth is violated — that is RP-6's actual cost and it is a test-suite defect, not a reasoning defect.**

**What AM-33 is NOT:** not a re-opening of AM-19, whose central claim survived a direct cross-vendor attack, and not a change to the list bound. **The list bound's value of 500 is CONFIRMED and re-derived here on the ground Amendment 3 lacked:** the list bound is **cheap** (≤5 requests) while the cost ceiling is **expensive** (≈100), so equalising them — the obvious "simplification" — would trade a nearly-free ordering-completeness guarantee for an expensive one and re-open AF-8. **They must stay different, and 500 gives multiple years of headroom at the observed growth rate against a population of 98.**

### AM-34 — **RP-3(a). The halt is CORRECT and it stays. The defect is that it is MUTE, and that is fixed here.
