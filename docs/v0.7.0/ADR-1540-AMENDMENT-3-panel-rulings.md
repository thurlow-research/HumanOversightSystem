## 1. Rulings

### AM-19 — **P-1, THE RULING. The defect is the evaluation *order*, not the cap's value. The gate MUST rank first and evaluate authorization lazily in rank order; the fixed authorization-check cap is WITHDRAWN as a coverage budget and replaced by a cost ceiling that can only ever truncate the *tail of the priority order*.** (Amends **AD-3**'s error-exit refinement; amends TD §2.2's `--max-authorization-checks` semantics and §2.3 Steps D5.1 and D6; confirms **AM-14**, whose stated basis this repairs.)

In one sentence, because the sentence is the ruling and everything after it is reasoning:

> **Rank every candidate before spending a single authorization check, walk the ranked list, stop when enough eligible candidates have been found or when the cost ceiling is reached, and never admit a candidate found after the stop — so that whatever the gate emits is always an exact prefix of the answer a complete walk would have produced.**

**Why the cap's *value* was never the defect.** Ranking is free: `priority:*` is a label, labels are in the Step C list payload, and D6's rank function already reads nothing else. Spending a paid operation before a free ordering operation is the error. Measured against the live queue: there are **3** `priority:critical` records among 97 (#1539, #1643, #1678), and the walk's key is `(rank, number)` ascending, so under a ranked walk **#1539 is the FIRST record evaluated** — and third at absolute worst, if the tie-break within a rank were reversed. Inside a cap of 25, inside a cap of 5, inside any cap a cost model would plausibly choose. It is evaluated at position 88 of 97 under the design as written. The panel's "not fixable by raising the cap" is correct and its converse is the interesting half: **once the order is right, the cap almost stops mattering** — which is the strongest available evidence that order, not budget, was the defect.

**Why ranking alone is not enough, and the second half of the ruling exists.** With a cap of 25 in rank order, the gate evaluates the 3 criticals and 22 of the 58 highs. If all 25 are gated and the human has authorized the 30th, the gate reports `eligible=0`, emits `ALL-CANDIDATES-GATED`, and the authorized issue is never built. That is P-1 moved, not removed — a smaller region, the same permanent unselectability, the same absence of any signal naming it. **So the ceiling must not bind in normal operation**, and when it does bind, what it drops must be the lowest-ranked records and it must say so.

**Binding — the algorithm, replacing TD §2.3's Step D5.1 and re-ordering D6:**

1. **D1, D2, D3, D4 are unchanged and remain free** — PR filter, `EXCLUDED_LABELS`, the requester verdict from the list payload, and the trusted-author fast path. None costs an API call.
2. **Rank the whole candidate set before any authorization check** (today's D6 rank function, unchanged: `priority:critical`→0, `high`→1, `medium`→2, else 3), ordered by `(rank, number)` — the identical total order Step E already sorts by. This is now **Step D-rank** and it precedes D5.
3. **Walk the ranked list in order.** A trusted record (D4) is eligible at zero cost. An untrusted record costs one authorization check (D5, unchanged in its internals — AM-4's title binding, AM-5's page bound, AM-18's milestone-object skip and D5.3's exit-2 on a failed events query all stand exactly as written).
4. **Sufficiency stop.** When the eligible set reaches `--max-candidates` (**new flag; default 10; may only lower**), stop the walk. `bin/hos-cron` consumes five (`head -5`); ten is deliberate headroom so that the stop is never the thing that decides *which* issue is built.
5. **Cost ceiling.** `--max-authorization-checks` **survives as a name and changes meaning**: it is a cost ceiling, not a coverage budget. **Default 100** — one check per record on one list page, i.e. the natural per-cycle maximum — and, as before, it **may only be lowered**, never raised: `min(100, max(0, provided))`.
6. **On the stop, the walk ends completely.** Today's D5.1 skips "this and every remaining *untrusted* record" and keeps admitting trusted ones. Under a ranked walk that is wrong: a trusted record at rank position 90 would be emitted while an authorized record at position 30 was never evaluated, which is precisely the *"silently demote a `priority:critical` item"* hazard D5.3 refuses to accept. **Binding: when either stop fires, no further record is admitted, trusted or otherwise.**

**The property that makes a partial answer safe, stated precisely, because §3 row F15 got it wrong in a way worth naming.** F15 accepts the cap as safe because *"a partial answer is a subset of the eligible set."* A subset is **not** sufficient; a **prefix** is. Under this ruling every early stop truncates a totally-ordered walk, so the emitted list is byte-identical to the first *k* lines a complete walk would have emitted, and nothing omitted can outrank anything emitted. Under the cap-in-API-order it was an arbitrary subset of an unrelated order, so the emitted list was not a prefix of anything and a `priority:critical` record could sit — did sit — entirely outside it. **"A subset is always safe" is the reasoning §9/T1 asked the panel to find the exception to. The exception is that it is not a subset property at all; it is an ordering property.** That sentence is the whole of P-1 and it should be carried into the design in those terms.

**The cost, measured rather than asserted, because a ruling that raises operating cost without pricing it is not a ruling.** Worst case per cycle after this ruling: 1–5 list requests + up to 100 events requests (each `gh api --paginate`, bounded to 10 pages by AM-5; the measured maximum event count on any issue in this repo is **17**, i.e. one page) ≈ **105 requests**. At the documented five-minute worker cadence (`docs/OVERSIGHT-RUNBOOK.md:56`) that is ≈ **1,260 requests/hour** against a 5,000/hour installation limit. The worst case is reached only when nothing is eligible — which is exactly the post-merge, pre-drain state AM-14's lazy drain creates, so it should be treated as the expected state for some weeks, not as a tail. **It is also the state in which the worker is doing nothing else and its API budget is otherwise idle.** The old cap held this to ≈25 checks by buying the reduction with correctness.

**The lever, named so nobody looks for it in the wrong place.** This cost is **FR7's** price, not the cap's: "no state file, no memo" means every cycle re-derives every authorization from live events. If the cost is ever judged unacceptable, the thing to revisit is FR7 (a pm-agent/human decision about whether an authorization may be memoised, and under what invalidation rule) — **not** the ceiling, which would buy the saving back in exactly the coin P-1 was paid in. Any future proposal to lower the ceiling below the candidate population must cite this paragraph and explain what it is trading.

**Disclosed, not absorbed.** The step from ≈25 to ≈100 paid calls per cycle is a change to the operating-cost envelope of the work-selection path, so per the CORE product-boundary checkpoint it is **disclosed to the human at H1** (§3(b), item 2's new cost bullet) rather than bound silently on the grounds that it is "just architecture". The technical call is mine; its consequence is theirs to clear.

### AM-20 — **P-1, aggravating detail 1. The gate's counts MUST distinguish a *determination* from an *absence of one*, and AM-13's held-everything line may never claim a completeness the walk did not have.** (Amends **AM-13**; amends TD §1.2's closed reason-token set and §2.3 Step F.)

The panel found that §1.2's reason-token set has **no token for a record the cap skipped**, and that the design does not say whether such records count toward `gated=<G>`. Both outcomes are wrong: counted, `gated` overstates the human's queue and mislabels a starved issue as *"held for human authorization"*; uncounted, the one load-bearing signal in the S2 era silently undercounts. AM-13 made that line load-bearing; a load-bearing line that can be wrong in two directions is not a signal.

**Binding:**

1. **`gated=<G>` counts only records for which the gate reached a determination that they are held.** A record the walk never reached is **not** gated; it is **unevaluated**.
2. **The summary line carries the walk's completeness explicitly**, not by inference from two numbers:
   `select_work_candidates: repo=<slug> milestone=<n> scanned=<N> evaluated=<E> eligible=<M> gated=<G> unevaluated=<U> auth_checks=<A> complete=<yes|no>`
3. **Three new reason tokens join §1.2's closed set, and they are unevaluated-reasons, not gate-reasons** — `unevaluated:sufficient`, `unevaluated:cost-ceiling`, `unevaluated:list-truncated` (AM-22). They are reported on their own line and MUST NOT appear in the `ALL-CANDIDATES-GATED reasons:` breakdown, which enumerates determinations only. `no-codeowner-actor` remains the gate-local D5.5 token it is today.
4. **AM-13's line still fires when `eligible == 0 and gated > 0`** — that is unchanged and remains required; operationally "the worker has no work" is the fact the operator needs at 3am. **But when `complete=no` it MUST say so in the same block**, naming the unevaluated count and its reason. A line that says *"<G> of <N> in-milestone issues are held for human authorization"* when *<U>* of them were never looked at is a false statement in the one place the design put its trust.
5. **The `complete=no` condition is itself operator-visible.** When the walk ends on the cost ceiling — as distinct from the sufficiency stop, which is routine — the gate emits a distinct `WARN authorization-cost-ceiling-reached evaluated=<E> unevaluated=<U>` line. Hitting the ceiling means the gate's answer is bounded by budget rather than by the queue, and that is an operational condition, not a detail.

**Why this is not logging polish.** AM-13 exists because a gate that holds everything and whispers is indistinguishable from an empty backlog. P-1 adds a third state the design had no vocabulary for: *a gate that did not finish looking.* Three states, two of which are silent, is worse than the two-state problem AM-13 was pulled forward from S6 to fix.

### AM-21 — **P-1, aggravating detail 2. The document argues both sides because it never wrote down the principle. Here it is: incompleteness that is UNKNOWN in extent exits 2; incompleteness that is KNOWN, ORDERED and REPORTED exits 0. D5.3 is unchanged; D5.1 is replaced by AM-19 and now satisfies it.** (Amends **AD-3**'s *"any error → exit 2"* refinement by giving it a stated criterion; confirms **F13**; replaces **F15**.)

The panel is right that §2.3 contradicts itself: D5.3 exits 2 on a failed events query because *"a partial candidate list is a correctness hazard — it can silently demote a `priority:critical` item"*, while D5.1 presents a partial determination as complete with exit 0 and demotes a `priority:critical` item. Both sentences were defensible locally; neither was written against the other. The resolution is not to pick a side — it is to state the property that distinguishes them, which neither passage did:

> **A bound may produce a partial determination with exit 0 only when (i) the omission is *enumerable* — the gate can state how many records it did not evaluate and why — and (ii) the omission is *ordered* — it falls at the tail of an order the gate itself controls, so nothing omitted can outrank anything emitted. If either property is absent, the determination is unknown in extent and the gate MUST exit 2.**

Applied:

- **D5.3 (events query fails): exit 2, unchanged.** The failure lands on an arbitrary record mid-walk. The gate cannot say whether that record would have ranked first, and continuing would emit a list that is not a prefix of anything. Neither property holds. **F13 stands exactly as written**, and its reasoning is now the general rule rather than a local argument.
- **D5.1 under AM-19 (cost ceiling): exit 0 + report.** Enumerable (`unevaluated=<U>`), ordered (the ranked tail). Both hold.
- **Sufficiency stop: exit 0 + report.** Same, and it additionally cannot coexist with the `ALL-CANDIDATES-GATED` condition, since it requires `eligible ≥ 1`. The one place AM-13's counts are load-bearing is therefore always a complete-or-ceiling walk, never a sufficiency-truncated one.
- **The list-page bound (AM-22): exit 0 + report, and it is the uncomfortable case, so I state it rather than smuggle it.** It is enumerable but **not** ordered — the page truncates by `created desc`, so a `priority:critical` record can be in the dropped region. Strictly, my own criterion pushes it toward exit 2. I rule exit 0 anyway, for one reason and with one obligation. The reason: exiting 2 there converts a large backlog into a **repo-wide stop** with no recovery act available to anyone, which is the denial-of-service argument D5.1 deployed in the wrong place and which is valid here. The obligation: because it is unordered truncation, its report is the **loudest class** — a distinct `WARN candidate-list-truncated` naming the bound, and AM-22 sets the bound high enough that reaching it is an operational emergency rather than a residual. **An architect who uses "it would stop the repo" to wave away an unordered truncation, and does not then make the truncation impossible in practice, has not ruled anything.** That is why AM-22 exists and why R-6 does not survive this amendment.

### AM-22 — **P-1, aggravating detail 3, and AF-8. The bounds get an architectural home, a stated purpose and a cost envelope. The list query is PAGINATED; R-6 is CLOSED, not carried.** (Amends **AD-3**; closes residual **R-6**; adds the bound set to **AD-10**'s failure-mode decision.)

The panel's third aggravator is that the cap **has no FR and no AD** — AD-10's per-cycle cap is the *assessor's* (ESC-5), and this one was introduced by the technical design at self-rated MEDIUM confidence, "not derived from measurement", and was never put to the human, while Amendment 2 §3(b) H1 item 2 assures the human that the queue's true size *"is knowable only by running the gate, which is what S2 ships."* That assurance was **not true** under a cap of 25: a gate that evaluates 25 of 97 records cannot report the queue's size. The panel is right that this is the sharpest of the three, because it is the point at which a design-stage number reached the human's clearance text wearing an architect's authority.

**Binding — the gate has exactly three bounds, they are decisions of mine, and each has a stated purpose:**

| Bound | Value | What it bounds | What it may truncate | Report |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **List pages** | `--paginate`, **5 pages / 500 records** | The candidate *set* | Unordered (`created desc`) — the oldest | `WARN candidate-list-truncated` — loudest class (AM-21) |
| **Authorization cost ceiling** | **100** checks, may only lower | The *cost* of the determination | The ranked tail (AM-19) | `WARN authorization-cost-ceiling-reached` + `complete=no` (AM-20) |
| **Events pages per issue** | 10 pages / 1000 events | One record's determination | Nothing — the record is gated | `WARN events-page-bound-reached` (AM-5, unchanged) |

**On pagination — the ruling and its price.** Step C fetches a single unpaginated page today, preserved as R-6 on the stated grounds that it *"preserves today's documented 100-issue single-page ceiling exactly."* AF-8 shows that ceiling is three records away and moves toward the oldest issues monotonically. **Binding: Step C paginates, bounded at 5 pages / 500 records**, normalising concatenated page arrays with the helper §1.4 already specifies for events. The cost is ≤4 extra list requests per cycle — negligible beside the 100 the ceiling already permits.

The division of labour between the two bounds is the point, and it is worth stating so the next reader does not "simplify" one into the other: **pagination buys *ordering completeness* — it is what lets a `priority:critical` record filed 150 issues ago be ranked at all; the cost ceiling buys *cost containment*; and the ranking is what makes the ceiling safe.** Dropping pagination re-opens AF-8. Dropping the ranking re-opens P-1. Raising the ceiling above the population buys nothing and costs rate limit. They are one design, like AM-18's skip rule and AM-4's title derivation, and the same warning applies.

**On the human's assurance.** With this ruling, Amendment 2 §3(b) H1 item 2's sentence — the queue's true size *"is knowable only by running the gate"* — **becomes true**, because the gate now evaluates the ranked set to the ceiling and reports `scanned`, `evaluated`, `gated` and `unevaluated` explicitly (AM-20). It was not true as designed. **That sentence is retained in §3(b) only because this amendment makes it true**; had I ruled differently on P-1 it would have had to be struck, and I record that so nobody later reads its survival as evidence it was fine all along.
