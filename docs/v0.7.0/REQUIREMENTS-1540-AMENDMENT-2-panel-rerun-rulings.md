## 1. AR-7 — THE RULING on **RP-1**. The `milestoned` channel is **REMOVED** from the authorization disjunction. The dispatch label, applied by a verified individual human CODEOWNER from their own account, is the **only** authorizing signal in the S2 era. (Narrows **#1539 ruling item 1**; amends **AM-15**'s surviving milestone clause; held for **Q11**.)

> **A `milestoned` event MUST NOT authorize anything.** An authorization is established by exactly one signal: a `labeled` event, for the dispatch label being queried, whose GitHub-reported actor is a verified individual human CODEOWNER. A milestone assignment — by any actor, to any milestone, at any time — is a **scheduling** act and carries no authorization weight. **FR28's per-request property is unchanged; what changes is the set of acts that can establish an authorization at all: it is now one act, on one channel.**

This is the panel's option (a), and I rule it for five reasons in ascending order of weight.

**1. The disjunction has no constituency: it is carrying nothing.** §0.2 measures it directly — **0 of 98** candidates are authorized by the milestone channel alone. Every channel-two authorization in the live queue is redundant with a channel-one authorization on the same issue. A channel that authorizes nothing that is not already authorized is not a capability being removed; it is an **unused second door on a vault**, and RP-1 is the observation that the second door has a weaker lock.

**2. The human's documented procedure already excludes it, and the shipped documentation is about to *say so in terms*.** AM-15 forbids the milestone route as an authorization procedure. AM-27 point 2 lands that prohibition in `docs/LABELS.md`, and TD `:1126` makes it **required statement 5**: *"the milestone route is FORBIDDEN as an authorization route."* **Under the current design that sentence is false about the gate** — AM-15 `:133` keeps the milestone channel live *inside* it, so a human who reads `docs/LABELS.md`, believes the milestone does not authorize, and then re-milestones an issue during planning has authorized it while believing they did not. **That is an FR29 breach on its face**: FR29 requires every artefact that tells a CODEOWNER how to perform the act to state plainly *"what the act does not do."* A mechanism whose own required documentation contradicts it cannot satisfy FR29 by writing the documentation more carefully; the contradiction has to go. **AR-7 removes it by making the documentation true.**

**3. The ergonomic cost is zero, act-for-act, and I checked each case rather than asserting it.**
- *Issue already in the target milestone and already dispatch-labelled (every issue in the live queue).* Before: the milestone route requires remove-then-re-add of the milestone — 2 acts, and **forbidden by AM-15** because Step 0 sweeps milestone-less issues. After: the label route, remove-then-re-add — 2 acts. **No change.**
- *Issue triaged by Step 0 but not yet authorized.* Same as above. **No change.**
- *Milestone-less, unlabelled issue a human is triaging by hand.* Before: assigning the milestone was 1 act and authorized it. After: applying the dispatch label is 1 act and authorizes it. **Same cost; a different act** — and the act is now unambiguous instead of doubling as a scheduling decision. **This is the only case AR-7 changes at all, and it changes it from "one act that means two things" to "one act that means one thing."**
- *Human-authored issues.* Untouched — they are trusted by authorship (**FR2**) and were never gated.

**4. The variant the panel found is routine, is instructed by this repo, and no wording can reach it.** Worker Step 0 triage assigns milestone **and** dispatch label together, both as a bot — correctly unauthorized. The human then re-routes the issue to a different milestone, which this repo's own triage rules actively instruct (`v0.5.1` drained, `v0.6.0` closed, priority overrides theme). That writes a fresh `milestoned` event under the human's account; AM-4's byte-for-byte check compares it against the milestone now under consideration, and it **matches**. The issue is authorized. **AM-15's prohibition does not reach this**, because AM-15 addresses the human's *deliberate* use of the milestone as an authorization route, and this is their *routine* use of it as a milestone. H1 item 2's bullet — *"do not use the milestone route"* — has the same gap. **You cannot instruct someone out of a side effect of an act they are performing for its intended purpose.** The only instruction that would work is *"never re-milestone an issue"*, which contradicts the repo's planning process. **The channel has to go, not the wording.**

**5. Dispositive on direction: the narrowing runs in the fail-closed direction, and it costs the human's ruling nothing it was for.** #1539's headline is *"No work happens without approval from the designated human (CODEOWNERS)"* — a **necessary** condition. Item 1's *"and/or"* names the **signals to check**, in a sentence whose stated purpose is *"bring `STRATEGY_MILESTONE` up to actor verification"*; item 2 supplies the rule's real content (*"a bot applying the label does not count"*). **Narrowing "and/or" to "and-only-the-label" removes a way to *grant* authorization; it removes no protection and it builds nothing the ruling forbade.** Every issue the ruling intended to gate stays gated; 92 of 98 candidates are gated either way, and the 6 that are not stay authorized on the human's own label acts. **There is no reading of the ruling under which the outcome gets worse.**

### What AR-7 is NOT

- **Not a change to the trust determination.** FR4-as-amended, FR1/FR2/FR3/FR5/FR6 and AM-1's option C are untouched. This is layer 2 only.
- **Not a ruling on *how* the design expresses it.** Whether `expected_milestone_title` disappears, whether the `milestoned` arm of `_verify_codeowner_actor` is deleted or permanently passed the "ignore" value, whether AM-4/AM-5/AM-18's milestone clauses are retired or left inert — **all of that is the architect's and the designer's.** I rule that the milestone channel confers no authorization; I do not rule a line of code. §5 routes it.
- **Not a claim that the milestone is irrelevant.** The milestone still selects the *candidate set* (Step C). It simply cannot *authorize* a member of it.
- **Not self-ratifying.** See **Q11**. AR-7 fails closed, so the chain may build on it before ratification — a "no" from the human restores a channel, it does not un-build one.

## 2. AR-8 — What AR-7 does **not** close: residual **R-3** becomes the only stale-authorization path, and it is live. (States, rather than changes; routed to `architect`.)

A narrowing invites the reading that the class is closed. It is not, and this section exists so the chain does not inherit that reading.

**The gate authorizes on *history*, not on *current state*.** `probe.py:315-322` walks the event list backwards and `continue`s past bot actors rather than stopping at them, so **a CODEOWNER `labeled` event authorizes forever, even after a bot has removed the label and re-applied it.** TD `:395` specifies exactly this and TD `:413` records the consequence — *"Events for `unlabeled`/`demilestoned` are ignored in S2"* — as residual **R-3**, accepted for S2 by **AM-8** and closed in S3.

**Three statements, each of which the chain needs and none of which is currently written down:**

1. **R-3 is written for the milestone channel only.** TD `:1566` states it as *"a CODEOWNER `milestoned` event that was later reversed … and re-applied by a bot."* **AM-8's binding S3 rule (`:164`) correctly covers both channels** (`demilestoned` **or** `unlabeled`), so the S3 fix is sound — but the S2 *residual* as stated names the channel AR-7 deletes. **After AR-7, R-3's stated instance ceases to exist and its unstated instance is the whole of it.** A residual that describes a channel that no longer exists is a residual that gets closed as obsolete by the next reader. **Binding on `architect`: R-3 must be restated for the dispatch-label channel.**
2. **R-3 is live, and #1542 is the instance** (§0.2). The human applied `needs-ai` on 2026-09-10; a bot removed it 15 minutes later when the issue went to `needs-human`; a bot re-applied it on 2026-09-15. The gate authorizes it on the 2026-09-10 human event. **This is materially the same shape AM-8 accepted** — the human did genuinely perform the act at some point — and I am **not** overturning that acceptance; the availability argument for it (`AMENDMENT-1:160`) is sound and unmeasured in the other direction. **But AM-8 accepted it in the abstract and it now has a name, a number and a date, and that belongs in the S2 work item and in front of the human** rather than being discovered later. The panel's §5 item 2 asked that #1542 be re-checked after any RP-1 ruling; **this is that re-check, and its result is "#1542 is an R-3 instance, not an RP-1 instance."**
3. **The docstring at `probe.py:292-294` is wrong about its own function** (§0.1, Correction 1) — it says "most recent" where the code says "any, ever". This is **shipped code in the authorization path**, it misled a cross-vendor adversarial panel into measuring the wrong query, and it is the kind of error `AMENDMENT-3` AM-28(c) ruled is *"not cosmetic in this chain."* **Recommended for the round-5 change set as a one-line correction**; if the architect judges it out of S2's scope, it should be filed rather than absorbed. **It is not mine to place.**

**Requirements position, stated once so the architect is ruling against something explicit:** **FR28** already says an authorization *"is re-derived live at every selection"* and is *"per-content-state"*. An authorization derived from an event whose effect has since been reversed is not re-derived live in any sense that FR28 means; it is a cache with an event log for a backing store. **I am not converting that observation into a new S2 requirement** — AM-8 weighed the availability cost and I have no measurement contradicting it, and adding a de-authorization rule to a `priority:critical` slice on round 5 of 5 would be exactly the scope creep this chain has otherwise avoided. **I record that FR28 and R-3 are in tension, that S3's AM-8 rule resolves it, and that the tension must be stated in the S2 work item rather than left for a reader to find.**

## 3. AR-9 — THE RULING on **RP-3(b)**. FR29 is **AMENDED**: it keeps its content obligation and gains an **existence** obligation, so that a deployment cannot receive the gate with no artefact describing the act. (Amends **FR29**; the consumer-shipping half is scoped and routed, not absorbed.)

**The panel's framing is right and I take the second branch of it:** *either FR29 is met for S2's documents, or AR-6 must be amended to say what it actually requires.* **AR-6's row, as I wrote it, does not say what it requires, and the defect is mine.**

**What I got wrong.** AR-6's FR29 row reads *"Delivered by AM-15's wording, `docs/LABELS.md`, and P-3's runbook fix. S2 MUST NOT merge without it."* I wrote that from inside this repository, where those three artefacts exist and are read. **FR29's own text binds "every artefact that tells a CODEOWNER how to perform it"** — a *content* obligation on a set of artefacts, which is **vacuously satisfied when the set is empty.** In a consumer deployment the set *is* empty: `framework_consumer_files.txt` names no `docs/` path at all (verified, §0.1), while **AM-26 part 1 puts the gate's two modules on that list**, so the consumer receives the control and none of its documentation. **A content obligation with no existence obligation is not a shipping condition; it is a style guide for documents that may not exist.**

**Where I agree with the designer, and where I do not — the panel asked me to rule on their triage and I do.** The designer judged "no `docs/` ships to any consumer, ever" a pre-existing repo-wide defect rather than one S2 creates, and declined to fold it into a `priority:critical` fix. **That triage is correct on scope and I uphold it.** The general defect is not S2's, it predates this chain, and fixing the installer's documentation-shipping story inside the fix for #1539 would be the worst kind of scope expansion — on round 5, on a security slice, on a protected surface. **What I do not accept is the inference that FR29 is therefore discharged.** S2 is the change that *creates a consumer-facing authorization control*. Before S2, a consumer's missing docs described a gate that did not exist; after S2, they describe a gate that silently decides whether their worker does any work at all. **S2 is what converts a documentation gap into a control gap**, and a shipping condition is not satisfied by observing that the shipping mechanism was already broken.

### FR29 — REPLACEMENT TEXT (binding; supersedes `REQUIREMENTS-1540:556-568`)

> **FR29 — The authorizing act MUST be legible as an authorization to the human performing it, and at least one artefact stating it MUST reach every deployment that receives the gate.**
>
> **(a) Content obligation — unchanged.** Because the authorization is a **human judgement** — and until the assessment layer ships, the only judgement in the loop — every artefact that tells a CODEOWNER how to perform it MUST state plainly (i) that the act authorizes autonomous work to begin on that specific issue, (ii) that this **includes issues the CODEOWNER did not author and whose content they do not control**, and (iii) what the act does *not* do — it does not make the author trusted, and it does not make the issue body trustworthy. It MUST NOT be described only as a routing, triage or visibility action, and **it MUST NOT describe as authorizing any act that the gate does not treat as authorizing** (**AR-7**: a milestone assignment is not an authorizing act).
>
> **(b) Existence obligation — NEW.** **No deployment may receive the gate without also receiving at least one artefact carrying (i)–(iii).** For the **operating deployment** (this repository) that obligation is discharged by `docs/LABELS.md` and `docs/OVERSIGHT-RUNBOOK.md`. For a **deployment that receives the gate through `bootstrap/hos_install.sh`**, it is discharged **only** by an artefact whose path is named in `scripts/framework/framework_consumer_files.txt`, because that list is what defines a consumer's received set. Which artefact carries it is **not specified here** and is the architect's and the designer's to choose.
>
> *Verify (a):* a reader of each such artefact can state, without opening another document, that applying the dispatch label from their personal account starts autonomous work on that issue; no such artefact describes the act as routing-only; no such artefact describes a milestone assignment as authorizing; a conformance test pins the statements in their durable documentation home.
> *Verify (b), mechanically and at merge time:* **a test asserts that at least one path listed in `scripts/framework/framework_consumer_files.txt` contains statements (i), (ii) and (iii)** — in the same form as the `test_labels_doc_*` family already pins wording in `docs/LABELS.md`. The test fails if the list is edited to drop that path, and it fails if the statements are edited out of it. `coder` satisfies FR29(b) when that test exists and passes; the overseer checks FR29(b) by running it.

**Why (b) is a bounded obligation and not the repo-wide defect in disguise.** It does **not** require `docs/` to ship. It does not require the installer to change. It does not require `docs/LABELS.md` to be copied into consumer repos — which **AM-26 part 2's reasoning positively argues against**, since `cp_framework_file()` is an unconditional overwrite (`hos_install.sh:615-626`) and a consumer's own labels documentation is consumer-maintained. It requires **one** artefact, on a list S2 is already editing (H1 item 4 names `framework_consumer_files.txt` as part of surface 1's change set), to carry three sentences. **A satisfying route demonstrably exists inside S2's current scope** — the two gate modules are themselves on that list by AM-26 part 1, so an artefact that ships by construction is already there. **I name that only as evidence that (b) is satisfiable without new scope, not as the choice.** The choice is the architect's.

**What I considered and rejected: "do not ship the gate to consumers in S2."** It would satisfy FR29 trivially and defer the whole question. **It is not available, and AM-26's verification is why:** `framework_consumer_files.txt:20` ships `bin/hos-cron`, whose candidates block invokes `python3 -m scripts.framework.select_work_candidates`. A consumer upgrading to a release carrying S2 without the modules gets `ModuleNotFoundError` every cycle, with no `$LOG_PREFIX` and no `ALL-CANDIDATES-GATED` token. **Not shipping the modules is strictly worse than shipping them undocumented**, so this option is refused on the architect's own verified ground rather than on preference.

**RP-3(a) — the team-handle CODEOWNERS halt — is NOT mine and I do not rule it.** It is routed in §5. I note only the interaction: **if RP-3(a) is left as-is, FR29(b) is satisfiable and pointless for that consumer** — they will have an artefact telling them how to authorize a gate that exits 2 before it ever reads an authorization. The two halves must be answered in the same round or the documentation describes a control the consumer cannot run.
